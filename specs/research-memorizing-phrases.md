# Research basis for targeted drills

Reviewed 2026-10-10. This is the focused evidence record for the [accepted drilling decisions](decision-targeted-drilling.md). It does not establish the entire proposed app workflow as a tested or optimal teaching method.

Extended on 2026-10-11 by the [answer-grading and scheduling evidence review](research-answer-card-and-scheduling.md), which informs a [concrete design recommendation](design/answer-card-and-review-policy.md). The user wants a scientifically grounded method to use normally, with no learner-run validation or special assessment feature.

## Findings relevant to the decisions

| Primary source | What was studied and found | What it supports, and the limit |
|---|---|---|
| [Li & Boers (2025), exercise formats for collocation learning](https://doi.org/10.1111/modl.70007) | 56 ESL learners studied 32 verb–noun collocations. Study-before-practice with cued written gap-filling produced the best delayed scores among the tested conditions, with modest differences. The missing verb's first letter constrained other appropriate answers. | Introduce the target and constrain the response before testing it. The study used written collocation tasks, not spoken whole-phrase recall; it does not establish first-letter hints as our interface or test rotating masks. |
| [Strong & Boers (2019), retrieval versus trial and error](https://doi.org/10.1111/modl.12579) | Studying phrasal verbs before retrieving their particles outperformed guessing unknown particles and receiving feedback. Delayed performance was poor across conditions and the advantage shrank. | Teach before asking for recall; do not turn retrieval into guessing unfamiliar language. This was not a trial of spontaneous dialogue use. |
| [Macis, Sonbul & Alharbi (2021), spacing of collocation learning](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.system.2021.102649) | Spacing benefited deliberate collocation learning on form recall after three weeks. Results differed for incidental exposure. | Space deliberate practice over time; do not generalize one result to every kind of exposure or prescribe one interval sequence. |
| [Sonbul et al. (2025), equal versus expanding spacing](https://doi.org/10.1002/tesq.3364) | Equal and expanding schedules did not differ significantly in collocation recall; equal spacing performed better on recognition. | Expanding intervals are not established as universally superior. The app's scheduler remains a product choice. |
| [Hashizaki, vocalization and formulaic sequence learning](https://doi.org/10.1177/13621688231224040) | In a study of 32 Japanese learners, reading collocations aloud improved listening recognition of form and meaning relative to silent reading, including at a one-week delay. | Vocalization can support learning, but recognition results do not demonstrate independent spoken recall, pronunciation gains or automatic conversational use. |
| [De Jong & Perfetti (2011), repeated speaking and fluency](https://sites.pitt.edu/~perfetti/PDF/de%20Jong%20Language%20Learning.pdf) | In a small study, posttest fluency gains persisted for learners who repeated speech content in 4/3/2 practice. | Repeated speaking tasks can help fluency. Applying this to brief phrase or dialogue drills is an inference; it does not justify a universal response timer. |

The most directly relevant cue finding is Li & Boers' reason for supplying part of the answer: “to prevent learners from supplying appropriate responses other than the target verbs.” This supports the user's concern about ambiguous prompts. It does not mean every expression can be identified by its beginning.

## Product decisions to evaluate

- Select sufficient, meaningful cue chunks separately for each phrase, considering competing expressions. The cue may retain the beginning, middle, ending or a combination.
- Sometimes change which chunks are hidden while preserving target clarity, and ask for the whole spoken phrase every time.
- Combine these attempts with model comparison, repair, spaced returns and rehearsal of specific learned dialogues.
- Track assisted practice separately from independent recall, with one schedule across all variants of the target.
- Use AI to prepare and check materials or assist with focused feedback; evaluate both material quality and assessment errors.

The sources above do **not** directly test our cue-selection method, rotating masks, the complete spoken workflow, or AI-generated prompts and grading. These are reasoned design choices, not claims of a research-proven optimum. In particular, changing the hidden chunk should be evaluated for clarity and later recall, not assumed better merely because it adds variety.

Open-ended prompts can elicit many valid responses. They cannot establish failure to remember a particular phrase when the task has not sufficiently identified that phrase. Likewise, producing a phrase with visible chunks is evidence of supported production, not of wholly independent recall.

## Source hygiene

Fang, Elgort & Chen's 2024 paper on retrieval schedules and collocation knowledge is excluded: the [publisher record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/201FABD72088A7F590598A5973E5B499) marks it as retracted. Commercial-method descriptions and unrelated writing or reading findings are not used to justify this drill workflow.

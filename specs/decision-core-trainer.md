# Learning policy and honest evidence

Use evidence-informed defaults and ordinary self-ratings; the learner is not asked to tune parameters, run calibration or establish effectiveness.

## Scheduling choices

All lessons and selected lessons use due/new eligibility in the selected language. Prioritize oldest due targets, with up to 20 targets per session and at most five new targets filling remaining capacity. A due backlog may consume the whole budget. This limits new work without forcing early repetitions of every lesson phrase.

Use the pinned FSRS library with population parameters and desired retention 0.90. Not yet maps to Again, With effort to Hard, and Recalled to Good. With effort means correct retrieval with difficulty, never partial recall or recognition after reveal. No Easy button is needed. Disable library short steps and fuzzing because the app owns its repair queue. Let elapsed time and memory history determine intervals and lapse adjustments; there is no fixed ladder, universal one-day reset or interval ceiling.

After eligible failure/help, a repair needs five minutes and two distinct other finalized targets. Cap extra attempts at two; finish a short session without a waiting timer. Repairs and repeated dialogue turns retain the original review group. Only its first eligible finalized result updates the schedule; later success cannot rewrite the first result or repeatedly promote it.

Rotate a phrase's accepted prompts across reviews: prefer the prompt with fewer presentations, then the one presented longest ago, then stable IDs.

## Evidence that can change a schedule

The first response and its support/modality remain immutable across comparison, help and rerecording. Answer reveal snapshots the original self-report before exposure; capture snapshots before recording and keeps the prompt until completion. Post-response reference playback does not invalidate the earlier response. A hint, help, reference, recent lookup or full-script exposure before responding remains support even after hiding the answer again. Check support at response time as well as presentation.

Supported ratings retain the learner's choice without becoming FSRS recall observations. A never-assessed supported target gets an honest next-day learning check without an invented success or memory state. Existing due obligations remain unresolved after supported, technical, unclear or stale practice. Silent recall can supply self-reported memory evidence; recordings also remain self-assessment, with no automatic proof of pronunciation or fluency.

Interrupted capture and technical failure create no automatic forgetting grade. Administrative unclear-prompt reports exclude the pinned prompt revision without grading memory; they do not add a learner problem submenu. Lookup recordings attach to actual exposure receipts without inventing a review session. Full-script dialogue exposure remains support; repeated target turns share a review group.

## Why these defaults

[Research on L2 spacing](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12479) supports spaced returns, without selecting one universal interval ladder. [Variable-retrieval research](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2413511121) motivates rotating prompts. [Within-session retrieval research](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263116000280) motivates bounded repetition rather than endless retries. Sources and their limits are in [spacing and grading](research/spacing-and-grading.md) and [phrase prompts](research/phrase-prompts.md).

These principles inform the policy; the exact rotation, three buttons, repair limits and FSRS setting are engineering choices. The [FSRS documentation](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/py-fsrs) describes the scheduler, not evidence that it is optimal for this whole-phrase workflow. Predicted retention under a declared prompt is not conversational fluency or a guaranteed success rate.

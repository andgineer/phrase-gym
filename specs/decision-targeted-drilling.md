# Product scope, prompts, words and help

Phrase Gym trains selected words, fixed expressions and prepared dialogues toward ready spoken use. It is a personal trainer, with no open-ended conversation, AI role-play or learner effectiveness experiments.

Each section below ends with the evidence it rests on. Summaries, limits and how each source was checked are in [research](research/README.md). No study tests this complete workflow: these rules apply the cited findings and are design inferences.

## What is being retrieved

The response is one fixed phrase, said word for word and checked against the reference. A learner below C1 is better served by a few memorized standard phrases than by improvised wording, so an improvised answer counts as Not yet even when it would be acceptable English. Fixed expressions declare equivalents only when saying any of them meets the same learning goal. Word and frame targets identify the required sense or construction and the slots that may vary. Different meanings are different targets. Examples, prompt variants, lesson memberships and dialogue appearances share one target and schedule.

One preferred phrase is drilled for each conversational moment, such as answering "How are you?" or opening a meeting. When the learner meets two drilled phrases for the same moment, they delete one from its card. A second phrase answers the same partner line only when it belongs to a genuinely different moment, for example feeling great versus feeling tired.

A numbered source entry is not automatically one target. Preparation separates the learner's line from the partner's preceding line and declares what is fixed and what may vary, so a memorized example sentence is not mistaken for command of its frame.

## Card language

Each learning language has a card language: the learner's native language (Russian) or the language itself. It decides the language of phrase situations and word prompts. English and Serbian use the language itself; German uses Russian. The help language is set separately, under [help per language](#help-per-language).

The setting lives in Phrase Gym, and preparation follows it. A change applies to newly prepared material; existing items are prepared again on request and keep their progress. A single item whose prompt would not be understood in the card language may get a simpler prompt.

Evidence:
- A prompt works only when the learner understands everything in it except the target. The coverage thresholds measured for reading whole texts do not transfer to one short sentence as a number; they support this functional requirement ([lexical thresholds](https://nflrc.hawaii.edu/rfl/item-detail/206)). A German A1 learner cannot read a German situation.
- Translation combined with contrastive explanation is an effective vocabulary and collocation teaching tool ([Laufer & Girsai 2008](https://doi.org/10.1093/applin/amn018)), so a Russian prompt for German is a legitimate way to specify an intention while the forms are being learned.
- English-only cards for English are the learner's choice and match the goal of answering real situations directly. No cited study shows that translation-cued production is ineffective. See [card language](research/phrase-prompts.md#card-language).

## Prompts for fixed phrases

A prompt gives what a real conversation gives: a short situation in the card language and, where one exists, the other person's preceding line, played and shown. No part of the answer appears before the learner responds. The situation states facts and the communicative intent only; it never adds artificial register or emotion to single out one phrase. A phrase with no preceding line, typically an opener, is prompted by its situation alone ("Everyone has joined the call. Start the meeting.").

The learner's task is to say their phrase for that moment. Saying another drilled phrase that also fits is neither a memory failure nor a recall of the target: the learner deletes one of the two or recreates the card, and that attempt is not graded.

Each phrase starts with one prepared prompt. Prompts with genuinely different situations are added over time and rotated across reviews. The learner does not review prompts before use; reading prompts beside their answers would itself be study and spoil the first recall. A bad card is recreated from practice.

Evidence:
- Instruction with noticing plus oral practice answering a partner's turn raised oral use of conventional expressions: [Bardovi-Harlig, Mossman & Su 2017](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/12991145-c5d6-4eb5-b7c4-d2a228eb8d84/content), [Bardovi-Harlig, Mossman & Vellenga 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/1362168814541739). Their test accepted any taught routine of the right function, so it is precedent for the format, not proof that the format caused the gains. Recognition alone does not produce use ([Bardovi-Harlig 2009](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9922.2009.00525.x)). See [situation plus partner turn](research/phrase-prompts.md#situation-plus-partner-turn).
- Practice should include actual spoken production in the mode of use ([Li & Taguchi 2014](https://experts.nau.edu/en/publications/the-effects-of-practice-modality-on-pragmatic-development-in-l2-c/)). That study compared input-based with output-based practice, not translation with situation prompts. See [match practice to use](research/phrase-prompts.md#match-practice-to-use).
- Varied retrieval cues beat a repeated one ([Butowska-Buczyńska et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2413511121)). This supports varying the situation, not a particular number of prompts. See [vary the prompt](research/phrase-prompts.md#vary-the-prompt).
- No study compares partner-turn prompts with translation prompts for phrases, and recalling an opener from a written situation does not show that the learner will start a real call with it.

## Hints, first meeting and repetition

Hint is available only on request. It shows one partial cue chosen for the phrase, which may be any word including the first; a second request shows the full answer. A response after a hint is supported practice; how it affects the schedule is in the [learning policy](decision-core-trainer.md#evidence-that-can-change-a-schedule).

A phrase the learner has already studied, such as one from a completed course, starts with an ordinary unaided attempt. Preparation marks such material. A new phrase is first presented: the learner hears it, reads it with its situation and says it once with the reference, ungraded. The learner can always study instead of attempting by revealing the answer. After an attempt that was wrong or effortful, the comparison ends with saying the phrase once more with the reference; after a confident correct attempt the card advances.

Evidence:
- Where plain testing works, cues added within a trial as needed, cues diminished across trials and plain testing gave about the same benefit; cues added as needed helped most where plain testing failed ([Fiechter & Benjamin 2019](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-019-01617-6)). With feedback, fading cues were no better than plain testing ([Fiechter & Benjamin 2018](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/diminishing-cues-retrieval-practice-a-memory-enhancing-technique-/)). Less support during practice gives better later recall ([Carpenter & DeLosh 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16752591/)). No evidence identifies the best part of a multiword phrase to cue. See [least help](research/phrase-prompts.md#least-help-that-still-succeeds).
- For unfamiliar phrasal verbs, studying before retrieving beat guessing ([Strong & Boers 2019](https://doi.org/10.1111/modl.12579)), while gap-filling with an answer key and trial-and-error with answers afterwards gave similar retention ([Strong & Leeming 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/tesq.3248)). Errors followed by correction can help ([Metcalfe 2017](https://wesleyan.edu/ofcd/resources/OFCD%20Resource%20Docs/Learning%20from%20Errors.pdf)). Study first is therefore for unfamiliar material, not a blanket rule. See [study first](research/phrase-prompts.md#study-first-when-the-phrase-is-unfamiliar).
- Saying material aloud improves memory ([Hashizaki](https://doi.org/10.1177/13621688231224040), [Forrin & MacLeod 2018](https://uwaterloo.ca/news/news/study-finds-reading-information-aloud-yourself-improves)); nothing shows an extra say-along after every successful recall adds to it. See [say it aloud](research/phrase-prompts.md#say-it-aloud).

## Words

Every word item is spaced retrieval of one sense with one schedule, built around saying the word. Understanding happens when the word is first met: the learner hears and reads it in context with its meaning, then says it. Every review asks the learner to say it. Two or three short contexts rotate across reviews.

The prompt follows the card language:

- **The language itself** (English, Serbian): a short context with the word missing; the learner says the whole sentence. The answer shows a short definition in the help language and, where a confusable partner exists, a contrast note.
- **Russian** (German): the Russian meaning of a whole short chunk; the learner says the chunk. Tapping the word shows its gloss with article and plural. Fixed expressions are whole items, and a reply can be prompted by the other person's line. A translated meaning can have several correct German forms; preparation declares the accepted ones.

The learner grades the target word, sense or construction; an unrelated slip elsewhere in the sentence does not fail the word.

Confusable partners are lookalikes and near-synonyms that preparation marks within one inbox entry; the agent does not search existing items, so a pair arriving in separate entries is not linked, and its contrast notes still explain it. The second of a marked pair is introduced only after the first has been recalled once on a later day. After that both appear in ordinary reviews, with a contrast note that explains overlap as well as difference: *further* and *farther* both express distance, while only *further* fits "further notice". There is no separate mixed drill. Lessons are grouped by situation, not by semantic category.

Evidence:
- Flashcards ranked highest descriptively among intentional activities, but delayed form-recall differences between activities were not significant, and the results are aggregates of heterogeneous studies ([Webb, Yanagisawa & Uchihara 2020](https://doi.org/10.1111/modl.12671)). Retrieving the word–meaning link stays the core; the gap is one prompt format, not proof of independent use. See [flashcards versus context](research/words-in-context.md#flashcards-versus-context).
- Receptive and productive practice build different knowledge ([Webb thesis](https://openaccess.wgtn.ac.nz/articles/thesis/Investigating_the_Effects_of_Learning_Tasks_on_Vocabulary_Knowledge/16945963), [Griffin & Harley 1996](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/applied-psycholinguistics/article/list-learning-of-second-language-vocabulary/F00040624B9C1370140CF398F9A1E0B0)). Successful understanding must not stretch the interval of the schedule that saying depends on, so the schedule is built around saying from the first meeting. See [several contexts](research/words-in-context.md#several-contexts-practised-in-the-needed-direction).
- Several contexts beat one ([Bolger et al. 2008](https://www.lrdc.pitt.edu/perfettilab/pubpdfs/Context%20variation%20Bolger%20et%20al.pdf)).
- Related words learned together produced more interference errors with the same overall scores ([Nakata & Suzuki 2019](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263118000219)), and similar forms are a systematic error source ([Laufer 1988](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/the-concept-of-synforms-similar-lexical-forms-in-vocabulary-acqui/)): this supports a mild separation, not a strong rule. Interleaving helps with visual categories, but in the word studies blocking did better, g = −0.39 ([Brunmair & Richter 2019](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000209)), so mixed confusable drills have no support. See [confusable words](research/words-in-context.md#confusable-words).

## Fixing cards from practice

Every practice card has Delete and Recreate card:

- **Delete** removes the item from practice and lookup: a duplicate of another phrase for the same moment, or anything added by mistake. Its history and recordings stay in the database.
- **Recreate card** sends the item back through the [inbox](decision-external-preparation-agent.md#inbox) to be prepared again, with an optional comment on what is wrong. The learner does not classify the problem; a free comment tells the agent more than a category would. The reworked item keeps its progress.

An attempt on a card deleted or recreated before it is rated is not graded. To learn words seen on a card, the learner copies them and pastes them into the [drop space](decision-external-preparation-agent.md#inbox), the same way as text from any other app; cards get no selection button of their own.

## Help per language

Each learning language has its own help language and its own choice of whether the translation appears with the answer. English uses English help, no translation and no Russian. Its notes exist only where they add something (register, when the phrase fits, unusual words) and open from Explain; when a definition does not explain a word, help offers a simpler explanation or a contrasting example, not Russian. German shows the Russian translation with the answer and again after the recording comparison. Serbian uses no Russian; its help language is not yet set and is chosen with the learner before Serbian material is prepared. Show & explain appears only when help exists in that language's help language; missing help has no placeholder.

Evidence: translations helped weaker learners most, while context helped transfer to new sentences ([Prince 1996](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-4781.1996.tb05468.x)). Proficiency interacted with the testing language, not the study language ([Joyce 2016](https://www.nepjol.info/index.php/NELTA/article/view/20205)), so no study shows that stronger learners learn better from L2 definitions. The per-language choice rests on comprehensibility and the learner's preference. No study shows that an occasional native-language gloss harms an advanced learner. See [translation or L2 explanation](research/words-in-context.md#translation-or-l2-explanation).

## Keeping practice going

Game elements must themselves be useful practice:

- **Dialogue runs** go through a prepared dialogue: the other person's lines play, and the learner says their own lines from memory, taking all the time they need. There is no timer anywhere. After each learner line the reference plays and the learner rates it; the rating moves the run on to the next line. A dialogue is not scheduled as a unit; its learner lines are phrases with their own schedules. A line said without its text shown is an ordinary review of that phrase; after reading the full script, the run is supported practice.
- **Progress:** each lesson shows how many of its phrases were recalled without help at their last check and how many are overdue. It is dated evidence, not mastery.
- **Real calls** (optional): before a call, the learner picks two or three phrases for a concrete opportunity ("if I open the meeting, I'll use…"). Afterwards each is marked used, no chance or forgot. No chance is not a failure. The marks never change a schedule: the phrases were picked and read just before the call, so using them minutes later says little about long-term memory, and "forgot" usually means forgetting to use a phrase rather than failing to recall it.
- **Weekly target:** replaces a daily streak; a missed day costs nothing.

There are no points, badges or leaderboards.

Evidence:
- Game elements have small positive effects ([Sailer & Homner 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w)); a meta-analysis of heterogeneous interventions cannot identify the effective part of this design. Progress displays support the feeling of competence ([Sailer et al. 2017](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/files/109059/109059.pdf)).
- A leaderboard with required badges lowered motivation and exam scores ([Hanus & Fox 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2014.08.019)). Expected rewards for activity undermine intrinsic motivation ([Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999](https://leeds-faculty.colorado.edu/dahe7472/deci%201999.pdf)).
- The effect of game elements fades after about four weeks ([Rodrigues et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-021-00314-6)). Broken streaks cause drop-off ([Silverman & Barasch 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucac029)). Habits grew with repetition in a consistent context, and one missed opportunity did not disrupt them ([Lally et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)).
- If-then plans improve goal attainment ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1)).
- Speed needs more practice than accuracy ([Li 2013](https://benjamins.com/catalog/lllt.36.04li)), and repeating the same talk preserved fluency gains ([de Jong & Perfetti 2011](https://sites.pitt.edu/~perfetti/PDF/de%20Jong%20Language%20Learning.pdf)). Shrinking time improved fluency but not accuracy ([Thai & Boers 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/tesq.232)). The learner wants no time pressure, so speed is left to repeated spaced recall of the same lines.

See [motivation](research/motivation.md) and [automatic use](research/automatic-use.md).

## Interaction constraints

One Start session action uses all lessons or a selected lesson. Starting explicitly replaces the known open session while retaining its history; Resume preserves the existing position. Browsing or changing language never replaces a session. Phrase lookup searches text and prepared concepts across the language's lessons, opens reference material and links to complete lesson lists. Browsing alone never grades recall.

Keep one compact practice card with the prompt, optional recording, Hint, answer reveal, inline help, replay, Recreate card, Delete and three self-check choices. Ratings save and advance quietly. The microphone directly starts another take. No recording-mode switch, Practice again, supported-only Continue, saved-rating banner or Undo UI.

Learning language belongs to bookmarkable URLs and scopes content/session views. UI follows either native or current learning language, with no independent UI-language selector. Explicit URLs win over best-effort device restoration; navigation makes no Settings writes. Settings saves one complete preference set at a time per tab, retaining failed drafts. Live cross-tab Settings synchronization is outside scope.

Automatic stopping, speech grading, historical recording-comparison UI and locked-screen/eyes-free operation are deferred.

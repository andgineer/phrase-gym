# Product scope, prompts, words and help

Phrase Gym trains selected words, fixed expressions and prepared dialogues toward ready spoken use. It is a personal trainer, with no open-ended conversation, AI role-play or learner effectiveness experiments.

Each section below ends with the evidence it rests on. Summaries, limits and how each source was checked are in [research](research/README.md). No study tests this complete workflow: these rules apply the cited findings and are design inferences.

## What is being retrieved

The exercise retrieves a selected fixed phrase, said word for word and checked against the reference. This is a declared practice objective, not a claim that memorization is generally superior to improvisation below a proficiency threshold. An improvised answer does not demonstrate recall of the selected target even when it is acceptable language. Fixed expressions declare equivalents only when saying any of them meets the same learning goal. Word and frame targets identify the required sense or construction and the slots that may vary. Different meanings are different targets. Examples, prompt variants, lesson memberships and dialogue appearances share one target and schedule.

One preferred phrase is actively drilled for each communicative intention, such as answering "How are you?" or opening a meeting. Other useful expressions remain available for lookup rather than being deleted. Preparation checks bounded existing candidates; the learner can change which expression is preferred without merging meanings or erasing history. Changing preference removes the alternative from scheduled practice and invalidates its pending review bindings; browsing it remains study. A second phrase answers the same partner line only when it belongs to a genuinely different moment, for example feeling great versus feeling tired.

A numbered source entry is not automatically one target. Preparation separates the learner's line from the partner's preceding line and declares what is fixed and what may vary, so a memorized example sentence is not mistaken for command of its frame.

## Card language

Each learning language has a learner-chosen card language for phrase situations and word prompts, independently of its help language. [Personal preferences](decision-personal-preferences.md) owns the current learner's choices and their database/API lifecycle; Serbian prompts for this learner are Serbian, not a universal product default.

Preparation uses the pinned profile revision. A prompt must be comprehensible; simplify incidental wording rather than silently switching language. Existing items are re-prepared on request, preserving identity/history for the same meaning. Material changes to retrieval conditions require a new independent check before earlier recall evidence describes the changed task.

Evidence:
- A prompt works only when the learner understands everything in it except the target. The coverage thresholds measured for reading whole texts do not transfer to one short sentence as a number; they support this functional requirement ([lexical thresholds](https://nflrc.hawaii.edu/rfl/item-detail/206)). An unfamiliar German situation can overload an A1 learner; the chosen prompt language must make the task understood.
- Translation combined with contrastive explanation is an effective vocabulary and collocation teaching tool ([Laufer & Girsai 2008](https://doi.org/10.1093/applin/amn018)), so a Russian prompt for German is a legitimate way to specify an intention while the forms are being learned.
- English-only cards for English are the learner's choice and match the goal of answering real situations directly. No cited study shows that translation-cued production is ineffective. See [card language](research/phrase-prompts.md#card-language).

## Prompts for fixed phrases

A prompt gives what a real conversation gives: a short situation in the card language and, where one exists, the other person's preceding line, played and shown. No part of the answer appears before the learner responds. The situation states facts and the communicative intent only; it never adds artificial register or emotion to single out one phrase. A phrase with no preceding line, typically an opener, is prompted by its situation alone ("Everyone has joined the call. Start the meeting.").

The learner's task is to say their preferred phrase for that intention. A genuinely ambiguous prompt is recreated without grading memory; choosing another valid drilled expression is not successful retrieval of the selected target. Preference changes can retain alternatives for lookup. Accepted wording and variable slots are clear with the answer.

Preparation supplies up to two genuinely different natural situations where the same response fits, using one when a second cannot be defended. Do not manufacture differences or obscure cues to force an exact string. Rotate prompts across independent reviews and pin their modality and revision in each attempt. The learner does not approve prompts before use; a bad card is suspended and recreated from practice.

Evidence:
- Instruction with noticing plus oral practice answering a partner's turn raised oral use of conventional expressions: [Bardovi-Harlig, Mossman & Su 2017](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/12991145-c5d6-4eb5-b7c4-d2a228eb8d84/content), [Bardovi-Harlig, Mossman & Vellenga 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/1362168814541739). Their test accepted any taught routine of the right function, so it is precedent for the format, not proof that the format caused the gains. Recognition alone does not produce use ([Bardovi-Harlig 2009](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9922.2009.00525.x)). See [situation plus partner turn](research/phrase-prompts.md#situation-plus-partner-turn).
- Practice should include actual spoken production in the mode of use ([Li & Taguchi 2014](https://experts.nau.edu/en/publications/the-effects-of-practice-modality-on-pragmatic-development-in-l2-c/)). That study compared input-based with output-based practice, not translation with situation prompts. See [match practice to use](research/phrase-prompts.md#match-practice-to-use).
- Varied retrieval cues beat a repeated one ([Butowska-Buczyńska et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2413511121)). This supports varying the situation, not a particular number of prompts. See [vary the prompt](research/phrase-prompts.md#vary-the-prompt).
- No study compares partner-turn prompts with translation prompts for phrases, and recalling an opener from a written situation does not show that the learner will start a real call with it.

## Hints, first meeting and repetition

Hint is available only on request. It shows one partial cue chosen for the phrase, which may be any word including the first; a second request shows the full answer. A response after a hint is supported practice; how it affects the schedule is in the [learning policy](decision-core-trainer.md#evidence-that-can-change-a-schedule).

Default to new material. Mark a phrase already studied only from an explicit learner statement or a source fact establishing completed study, retaining that provenance; mere presence in a course is insufficient. Familiar material starts with an unaided attempt. A first encounter without a memory state can reveal into study without inventing a failed recall. New material is heard, read with its meaning/situation and said once with the reference, ungraded. Next completes that presentation without certifying speech. Its first independent recall can follow the repair spacing or occur in a later session; a short session never waits for it.

The learner can choose study before attempting; reveal after an attempted response preserves that original response for self-rating. After a wrong or effortful attempt, say the phrase once more with the reference, then use Next to finish the supported repetition. A confident correct attempt advances without an extra say-along. Recording stays optional and no speech-completion detector is implied.

Evidence:
- Where plain testing works, cues added within a trial as needed, cues diminished across trials and plain testing gave about the same benefit; cues added as needed helped most where plain testing failed ([Fiechter & Benjamin 2019](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-019-01617-6)). With feedback, fading cues were no better than plain testing ([Fiechter & Benjamin 2018](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/diminishing-cues-retrieval-practice-a-memory-enhancing-technique-/)). Less support during practice gives better later recall ([Carpenter & DeLosh 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16752591/)). No evidence identifies the best part of a multiword phrase to cue. See [least help](research/phrase-prompts.md#least-help-that-still-succeeds).
- For unfamiliar phrasal verbs, studying before retrieving beat guessing ([Strong & Boers 2019](https://doi.org/10.1111/modl.12579)), while gap-filling with an answer key and trial-and-error with answers afterwards gave similar retention ([Strong & Leeming 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/tesq.3248)). Errors followed by correction can help ([Metcalfe 2017](https://wesleyan.edu/ofcd/resources/OFCD%20Resource%20Docs/Learning%20from%20Errors.pdf)). Study first is therefore for unfamiliar material, not a blanket rule. See [study first](research/phrase-prompts.md#study-first-when-the-phrase-is-unfamiliar).
- Saying material aloud improves memory ([Hashizaki](https://doi.org/10.1177/13621688231224040), [Forrin & MacLeod 2018](https://uwaterloo.ca/news/news/study-finds-reading-information-aloud-yourself-improves)); nothing shows an extra say-along after every successful recall adds to it. See [say it aloud](research/phrase-prompts.md#say-it-aloud).

## Words

Every word item is spaced retrieval of one sense with one schedule, built around saying the word. Understanding happens when the word is first met: the learner hears and reads it in context with its meaning, then says it. Every review asks the learner to say it. Two or three short contexts rotate across reviews.

The prompt follows the card language:

- **The learning language:** a short familiar context with the word missing; the learner says the whole short sentence. Include a brief comprehensible sense cue when needed to avoid an ambiguous gap; it is part of the declared task, not an on-demand hint. The answer shows a short definition in the help language and, where a confusable partner exists, a contrast note.
- **Another chosen card language:** the meaning of a whole short chunk in that language; the learner says the chunk. For this learner, German uses Russian meanings. Tapping the word shows its gloss with article and plural where applicable. Fixed expressions are whole items, and a reply can be prompted by the other person's line. A translated meaning can have several correct German forms; preparation declares the accepted ones.

The learner grades the target word, sense or construction; an unrelated slip elsewhere in the sentence does not fail the word. Rotating contexts retain the same sense and construction, with understood surrounding grammar. For a beginner, keep the chunk short and understood rather than introducing a second receptive schedule or an arbitrary word-count limit.

Confusable partners are lookalikes and near-synonyms identified within the entry and through bounded queries of existing targets. Link justified candidates across entries, including separate word submissions, without merging based on similarity. Introduction dependencies follow the [learning policy](decision-core-trainer.md), including retirement and unavailable-prerequisite handling. After that both appear in ordinary reviews, with a contrast note that explains overlap as well as difference: *further* and *farther* both express distance, while only *further* fits "further notice". There is no separate mixed drill. Lessons are grouped by situation, not by semantic category.

Evidence:
- Flashcards ranked highest descriptively among intentional activities, but delayed form-recall differences between activities were not significant, and the results are aggregates of heterogeneous studies ([Webb, Yanagisawa & Uchihara 2020](https://doi.org/10.1111/modl.12671)). Retrieving the word–meaning link stays the core; the gap is one prompt format, not proof of independent use. See [flashcards versus context](research/words-in-context.md#flashcards-versus-context).
- Receptive and productive practice build different knowledge ([Webb thesis](https://openaccess.wgtn.ac.nz/articles/thesis/Investigating_the_Effects_of_Learning_Tasks_on_Vocabulary_Knowledge/16945963), [Griffin & Harley 1996](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/applied-psycholinguistics/article/list-learning-of-second-language-vocabulary/F00040624B9C1370140CF398F9A1E0B0)). Successful understanding must not stretch the interval of the schedule that saying depends on, so the schedule is built around saying from the first meeting. See [several contexts](research/words-in-context.md#several-contexts-practised-in-the-needed-direction).
- Several contexts beat one ([Bolger et al. 2008](https://www.lrdc.pitt.edu/perfettilab/pubpdfs/Context%20variation%20Bolger%20et%20al.pdf)).
- Related words learned together produced more interference errors with the same overall scores ([Nakata & Suzuki 2019](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263118000219)), and similar forms are a systematic error source ([Laufer 1988](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/the-concept-of-synforms-similar-lexical-forms-in-vocabulary-acqui/)): this supports a mild separation, not a strong rule. Interleaving helps with visual categories, but in the word studies blocking did better, g = −0.39 ([Brunmair & Richter 2019](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000209)), so a dedicated mixed-drill feature is deferred; this does not establish that every contrast activity is ineffective. See [confusable words](research/words-in-context.md#confusable-words).

## Fixing cards from practice

Every practice card has Delete and Recreate card:

- **Delete** confirms the named item and retires it from practice and ordinary lookup, preserving history and recordings. A Removed-items view can Restore it; this is not a transient Undo button. Retirement and restoration invalidate stale session bindings and resolve dependent introduction rules. Selecting a preferred alternative is separate from deletion.
- **Recreate card** immediately suspends the defective revision and records an inbox request with an optional comment and the expected target/revision. It remains suspended while preparation waits or fails, including when Phrase Gym Coach is offline. Compatible repair retains identity/history; a changed meaning creates a new target. Changed assessment conditions require an independent check. Show the preparation outcome without requiring the learner to classify the problem.

An attempt on a card deleted or recreated before it is rated is not graded. To learn words seen on a card, the learner copies them and pastes them into the [drop space](decision-external-preparation-agent.md#inbox), the same way as text from any other app; cards get no selection button of their own.

## Help per language

Each learning language has its own help language and translation-with-answer choice in [personal preferences](decision-personal-preferences.md). Notes appear only where useful (register, when the phrase fits, unusual words) and open from Explain. A circular definition gets a simpler explanation or contrasting example in the chosen help language. When enabled, translation appears with the answer and again after comparison. Show & explain appears only when help exists in that language; missing help has no placeholder. The external agent resolves required missing preferences through the inbox API before unattended preparation depends on them.

Evidence: translations helped weaker learners most, while context helped transfer to new sentences ([Prince 1996](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-4781.1996.tb05468.x)). Proficiency interacted with the testing language, not the study language ([Joyce 2016](https://www.nepjol.info/index.php/NELTA/article/view/20205)), so no study shows that stronger learners learn better from L2 definitions. The per-language choice rests on comprehensibility and the learner's preference. No study shows that an occasional native-language gloss harms an advanced learner. See [translation or L2 explanation](research/words-in-context.md#translation-or-l2-explanation).

## Keeping practice going

Game elements must themselves be useful practice:

- **Dialogue runs** go through a prepared dialogue: the other person's lines play, and the learner says their own lines from memory, taking all the time they need. There is no timer anywhere. After each learner line the reference plays and the learner rates it; the rating moves the run on to the next line. All full dialogue runs are rehearsal and never update FSRS, even without visible answer text: order, prior lines and reference playback can support recall. Retain their ratings and recordings. Independent due cards can use a situation and one partner turn, provided recent rehearsal has not supplied the answer.
- **Progress:** each lesson shows how many active preferred phrases were recalled without help at their last independent check and how many are overdue. Exclude retired targets and do not present old evidence as a check under materially changed prompts. It is dated evidence, not mastery.
- **Real calls** (optional): before a call, the learner picks two or three phrases for a concrete opportunity ("if I open the meeting, I'll use…"). Afterwards each is marked used, no chance or forgot. No chance is not a failure. The marks never change a schedule: the phrases were picked and read just before the call, so using them minutes later says little about long-term memory, and "forgot" usually means forgetting to use a phrase rather than failing to recall it.
- **Weekly target:** counts distinct targets with eligible independent reviews during the learning week; repairs and repeated runs cannot inflate it. A missed day costs nothing. Use the profile learning timezone.

There are no points, badges or leaderboards. No elapsed-recall display or hidden speed grade is added: UI timing includes reading, audio, permissions and interruptions, not just retrieval latency. Untimed spoken repetition may improve fluency, but this workflow does not prove automatic conversational use.

Evidence:
- Game elements have small positive effects ([Sailer & Homner 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w)); a meta-analysis of heterogeneous interventions cannot identify the effective part of this design. Progress displays support the feeling of competence ([Sailer et al. 2017](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/files/109059/109059.pdf)).
- A leaderboard with required badges lowered motivation and exam scores ([Hanus & Fox 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2014.08.019)). Expected rewards for activity undermine intrinsic motivation ([Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999](https://leeds-faculty.colorado.edu/dahe7472/deci%201999.pdf)).
- The effect of game elements fades after about four weeks ([Rodrigues et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-021-00314-6)). Broken streaks cause drop-off ([Silverman & Barasch 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucac029)). Habits grew with repetition in a consistent context, and one missed opportunity did not disrupt them ([Lally et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)).
- If-then plans improve goal attainment ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1)).
- Speed needs more practice than accuracy ([Li 2013](https://benjamins.com/catalog/lllt.36.04li)), and repeating the same talk preserved fluency gains ([de Jong & Perfetti 2011](https://sites.pitt.edu/~perfetti/PDF/de%20Jong%20Language%20Learning.pdf)). Shrinking time improved fluency but not accuracy ([Thai & Boers 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/tesq.232)). The learner wants no time pressure, so speed is left to repeated spaced recall of the same lines.

See [motivation](research/motivation.md) and [automatic use](research/automatic-use.md).

## Interaction constraints

One Start session action uses all lessons or a selected lesson. Starting explicitly replaces the known open session while retaining its history; Resume preserves the existing position. Browsing or changing language never replaces a session. Phrase lookup searches text and prepared concepts across the language's lessons, opens reference material and links to complete lesson lists. Browsing alone never grades recall.

Keep one compact practice card with the prompt, optional recording, Hint, answer reveal, inline help, replay, Recreate card, Delete and three self-check choices. In independent practice, ratings save quietly: Recalled advances; a wrong or effortful attempt finishes its supported say-along with Next. First-meeting study also uses Next without a grade. These are explicit completion actions, not speech verification. The microphone directly starts another take. No recording-mode switch, Practice again, saved-rating banner or transient Undo UI. Removed-items restoration remains available.

The three rating criteria describe the original response: Not yet means the target was missing/wrong or needed help; With effort means correct unaided but difficult; Recalled means correct unaided and comfortable. Freeze response modality/support before exposure, then judge that response after comparison; no second pre-reveal correctness rating is required.

Learning language belongs to bookmarkable URLs and scopes content/session views. UI follows either native or current learning language, with no independent UI-language selector. Explicit URLs win over best-effort device restoration; navigation makes no Settings writes. Personal preparation preferences are edited through the inbox API by an external agent, as defined in [personal preferences](decision-personal-preferences.md). Remaining core Settings save their own complete device/UI preference set per tab, retaining failed drafts; they cannot overwrite the personal profile. Live cross-tab Settings synchronization is outside scope.

Automatic stopping, speech grading, historical recording-comparison UI and locked-screen/eyes-free operation are deferred.

# Research evidence

Each note answers one design question. Every source has a link, what was studied, what it means for Phrase Gym and its limit. Notes summarize; they never copy papers. **Checked** states what was actually read: full text, abstract, or a secondary description. A secondary-only claim never justifies a decision on its own.

| Question | Note |
|---|---|
| What should the learner see or hear before saying a fixed phrase, and what happens after an error? | [Phrase prompts](phrase-prompts.md) |
| What makes phrases come out automatically in speech? | [Automatic use](automatic-use.md) |
| How should reviews be spaced and graded? | [Spacing and grading](spacing-and-grading.md) |
| How should words be learned with translation or explanation, context and contrast? | [Words](words-in-context.md) |
| Which game elements keep practice going without hurting learning? | [Motivation](motivation.md) |

No source tests Phrase Gym's complete spoken workflow. A decision that applies these findings is a design inference and says so.

Excluded: Fang, Elgort & Chen (2024) is retracted ([publisher record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/201FABD72088A7F590598A5973E5B499)). Vendor and blog descriptions (Pimsleur, EPI) and community anecdotes are not evidence.

## Findings

### 1. [P1] Recovery can overwrite a newer finalized take with its old capture snapshot

**Evidence:** `frontend/src/takeTransfers.ts:93-105` reads all takes before awaiting `liveCaptureOwners()`, then uses those stale values to delete zero-byte markers or replace capturing entries with incomplete pending takes. `frontend/src/captureOwners.ts:18-20` introduces a 200 ms asynchronous owner-discovery window. The final capture write at `frontend/src/composables/useCapture.ts:126-135` can update the same entry during that window. Neither recovery deletion nor recovery conversion rereads/compares the current entry in its write transaction. The subsequent `flush()` uploads the replacement and deletes it after a matching acknowledgement.

**Reachable reproduction:** tab B starts recovery while tab A has an unacknowledged capturing journal entry. During B's owner-discovery wait, A stops/releases capture and writes the final larger pending take, then exits before answering B's owner query. This also applies to interrupted finalization: the final incomplete bytes can be larger than the earlier snapshot. B regards A as absent and writes its old snapshot over the newly finalized bytes. If the earlier snapshot was empty, B deletes the newer take outright. A background/frozen owner that fails to answer within 200 ms can produce the same unsafe takeover while still alive.

**Confirmed independently in Chromium and WebKit:** a disposable browser harness seeded a valid take context backed by real present/respond receipts. It retained a 2,048-byte capturing snapshot, then deterministically wrote a 32,044-byte complete synthetic WAV under the same ID when production recovery sent its owner query, before the 200 ms wait ended. The fixture owner was absent. Production recovery overwrote that newer value, uploaded the 2,048-byte snapshot, and the real API/SQLite acknowledged `complete=false`, `byte_length=2048`. Both engines produced the same result. This deliberately controls the concurrent writer interleaving; it is not a claim of an actual-device recording/closure test. The final 32,044 bytes had already committed successfully to IndexedDB before recovery replaced them.

**Impact:** acknowledged audio can silently lose its final bytes, or a complete take can disappear after an empty stale marker is deleted. Matching ID/hash/size acknowledgements protect only the overwritten snapshot; they do not protect the newer evidence. An early partial upload also occupies the immutable server take ID, so a surviving writer's later final version conflicts instead of repairing the stored recording. This breaks the every-take retention and multi-tab recovery guarantees.

**Correction:** perform recovery conversion/deletion as a conditional IndexedDB read-and-write transaction against the current take, comparing the observed capture generation/version/status and retaining newer bytes. Do not interpret a short BroadcastChannel timeout alone as exclusive ownership of a live capture; use a cross-tab ownership mechanism that prevents recovery/upload racing an active writer, or a safe claim protocol with revalidation. Apply the same protection to zero-byte deletion and acknowledgement deletion. Add deterministic real-browser regressions for finalization during owner discovery, empty-marker replacement, and delayed owner response. Assert the final original ID/hash/byte count is retained and acknowledged exactly once.

### 2. [P2] Future exposure retroactively changes an earlier offline original response

**Evidence:** `src/phrase_gym/practice/evidence.py:33-35` considers a lookup exposure recent whenever its server `received_at` is at least `response_time - five minutes`; it never requires exposure time to be at or before the response. The on-card exposure check at lines 42-48 has the same missing upper bound. `create_attempt()` and `refresh_support()` (lines 125-129) use the earlier reconciled occurrence time, then `observe()` snapshots that computed support into the original response. `src/phrase_gym/practice/reviews.py:39-40` consequently suppresses the recall review. P4 makes this reachable through delayed, correctly timestamped offline outboxes.

**Reachable reproduction:** device A advances a downloaded session while disconnected and independently presents/rates the next target at time T1. Before A reconnects, device B looks up that target at T2, where T2 is later than the original response. B does not take over the session. When A submits its retained original IDs/timestamps, the server includes B's later exposure in A's earlier original support. A had never received that help before responding.

**Confirmed independently against the disposable real API:** create the three-target synthetic session; acknowledge target 1; retain target 2's present and recalled-rating events with their original timestamps; acknowledge a target-2 reference lookup afterward; then submit the retained events. Present occurred at `12:44:21.468435Z`, original response at `12:44:21.480954Z`, and the later exposure was acknowledged at `12:44:21.508875Z`. The server accepted the claimed occurrence time with `clock_adjusted=false`, but persisted `original.supported=true` and returned `supported_learning_check` instead of an eligible recall receipt. No content, schedule or session conflict was involved. The reproduction submitted the delayed outbox directly to the production endpoint; existing browser tests separately exercise the transport disconnection/replay path.

**Impact:** reconnection rewrites the meaning of the original offline response and can replace an eligible FSRS observation with a supported learning check; an existing target's due obligation can remain unresolved. The original support state shown before reconnect is no longer preserved. This is a concrete late-outbox/original-evidence defect, rather than an actual-device dependency or a request to restart generic P3 review.

**Correction:** bound both exposure checks to the interval ending at the original event's authoritative occurrence time, excluding exposure that happened afterward. Preserve the current rule that exposure between presentation and response can add support, and help after an already snapshotted original cannot relabel it. Add delayed-outbox tests with another-device lookup before versus after the response, including an existing scheduled target, plus the corresponding on-card exposure boundary. Use controlled clocks and verify original support, eligibility and schedule effects.

# P1 fixture and cue review

Fixture revision: `p1-2026-10-12.2`. Prepared on 2026-10-12.

These are authored prototype examples, not imports from the learner’s courses. The implementer compared each cue with the completions below. This is an editorial review; it is not user approval, a native-speaker certification or proof of uniqueness across an entire language. The ordinary drill assumes prior source exposure. Unfamiliar items can open Show & explain before responding.

All tasks ask for the complete phrase. Fixed-expression equivalents are listed; frame slots only change in a matching prepared situation. A valid sentence outside the selected wording is not automatically a memory failure. The word-use target supplies its construction and cannot receive word-recall credit. Rescue help adds exposure; a planned cue does not.

## Practice fixtures

### en-day

- Complete phrase: **How has your day been so far?**
- Language / lesson / objective: `en` / `rapport` / `fixed`.
- Meaning/use: A question about how today has gone up to this point. Present perfect links the earlier part of today with now.
- Explicit variants: How's your day been so far?
- Slots: None.
- Meaningful chunks: How has / your day / been so far?
- Reference: Whole-phrase macOS Samantha clip, en-US, prepared locally; a disposable feasibility voice, not a production choice.

- `en-day-cue-1`: **[…] your day been so far?**
  - Situation: Ask a colleague about today up to now. Recall the learned present-perfect question.
  - Competing completions: How was your day? (completed day); How is your day going? (different selected expression)
  - Review: Identifying anchors and a selected-expression context distinguish the learned target; complementary masks retain the same wording and purpose.

  - Situation: On an afternoon call, ask about your friend’s experience of today up to now. Recall the learned question.
  - Competing completions: How was your day? (completed day); How is your day going? (different selected expression)

## Counterexamples withheld from practice

- **broad** — Greet someone. Many valid answers; cannot grade one exact expression.
- **leak** — Could you look into […]? Reveals the objective in a word-retrieval task; usable only as word-use, not word recall.
- **unknown** — […] the bullet. Unfamiliar expression without established source exposure; show meaning before supported practice.
- **ambiguous** — Mogu li da dobijem […], molim vas? Without an order/bill situation, both coffee and bill fit. Context is required.

## Coverage and limitations

- `en-day` seeds earlier coverage of its ending only. The selector first hides the under-practised opening; equal coverage avoids immediately reusing the previous cue. This is purposeful chunk selection, not random masking.
- `en-touch` and `en-look` reuse their single reliable cue. The latter always hides `look into`.
- `en-name` includes contractions and middle anchors. `en-long` is the long-phrase case. English and Serbian frames include inflection restrictions.
- Two prepared dialogues reuse existing target IDs. Isolating a difficult turn preserves the exchange position; continuing restores the next partner line.
- Native Russian, English and Serbian meaning text is stored separately from learning-language situations. Serbian phrasing/translations remain subject to native-speaker review.
- The search index is hand-authored. `greetings` is concept-only in phrase wording; real retrieval quality is unvalidated. No embedding search or runtime generation is claimed.
- No actor/course clip was supplied. Local import and origin retention are implemented and exercised with a synthetic stand-in; an actual actor comparison remains pending.

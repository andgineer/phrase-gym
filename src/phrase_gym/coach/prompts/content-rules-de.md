### German cards

These notes apply only when the learning language is German. A beginner's cue gives the meaning
of a short chunk in the meaning-layer language and asks for the German chunk. The answer, the
revealed `text` and the wording fragments in hints stay German. The examples below assume a
Russian meaning layer.

- **Nouns:** practise a noun with its definite article, such as **die Tür**, and make the article
  part of the `target` and of the gap. When it helps, the explanation names the gender and the
  plural, for example **die Tür** (женский род, мн. ч. **die Türen**). When the gender depends on
  the sense, choose the sense from the context and give the article of that sense, as for
  **der Leiter** (руководитель) and **die Leiter** (лестница). Use cue frames in which the
  answer keeps its article and form as studied, usually as the subject: `__________ ist offen. (=дверь)`.
- **Capitalisation is part of the word.** A capitalised noun and a lowercase word with the same
  letters are different words, such as **das Essen** (еда) and **essen** (есть), or **der Arm**
  (рука) and **arm** (бедный). Keep the form the learner typed and never correct one into the
  other. A noun written in lowercase directly after an article, such as **die tür**, is an
  obvious typo for **die Tür**.
- **Separable verbs** split in a main clause: **einkaufen** becomes **Ich kaufe heute ein**. A gap
  covers one contiguous span only, so a cue for a separable verb uses a frame in which the verb
  stays whole, such as after a modal verb: `Ich muss heute noch __________. (=сходить за
  покупками)` for **einkaufen**. Never gap only the stem or only the prefix.
- **Beginner frames:** build the visible German frame from a few very common words, or give the
  gap alone with its gloss. The gloss translates the missing chunk as the frame uses it.
- **Hints** combine a German fragment of the target with a German companion, such as
  `T... / aufmachen` for **die Tür**, under the measured hint rules above.
